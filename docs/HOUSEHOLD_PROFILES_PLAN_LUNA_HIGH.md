## Intended experience

Peter and Maddie share one household dashboard. A persistent selector offers **Combined Budget**, **Peter**, and **Maddie** on every financial page. Peter and Maddie each have independent budget plans and financial records. Combined Budget shows the household total, with a visible breakdown into Peter, Maddie, and Joint.

**Combined is a view; Joint is an ownership designation.** Forms use “Peter,” “Maddie,” and “Joint (shared)” as ownership choices. Joint records appear in Combined, once, and are excluded from the personal views. Combined includes Peter + Maddie + Joint. Label this rule beside the selector on first use and in ownership help. Do not create an extra stored Combined total or a duplicate household copy of a record.

### Product defaults for this plan

- Both partners can view all three views and edit household financial records. Independent budgets mean independent planning and attribution, not secrecy between partners. A selector is not an authorization boundary. Private personal finances would require a separate permissions design.
- Keep exactly the three requested top-level choices. Within Combined, provide Peter/Maddie/Joint comparison columns and a Joint filter for editing shared budgets and reviewing shared activity.
- Combined initially opens as the household overview. Preserve the selected month when changing profiles; retain a valid current page. Display the active profile prominently on mobile too.
- Existing financial data migrates to Peter as a provisional classification, preserving current totals. Show an ownership-review checklist for existing accounts, properties, investments, and plans. Maddie and Joint start empty; never guess ownership from merchant names or institution names.
- Account ownership determines default transaction attribution; a reviewed transaction override may attribute a particular salary or expense to Peter, Maddie, or Joint. This handles Peter's salary deposited into a joint checking account without duplicating it or changing account ownership.
- V1 uses whole-record ownership. Percentage splits of a transaction between partners and automatic 50/50 allocation of Joint assets are deferred. Existing category splits remain supported and inherit the transaction's effective owner.
- Ownership reassignment moves the record and its dependent reporting history to the new owner after a preview and confirmation. Record before/after ownership and actor in an audit event. Household totals must remain unchanged. Historical reports use current attribution and say so; stored historical snapshots retain their original attribution and provenance. Effective-dated ownership is a later feature.
- Actual income/spending and planned income/budgets remain separate concepts. Do not add planned income to actual income or count transfers between household accounts as new income/spending.

These are implementation defaults, not claims that the user requested private access restrictions or percentage allocation. Confirm any changed product preference before implementing the affected behavior. Real Tailscale identities must be supplied through local configuration; never infer Maddie's login.

## Domain contract

Use stable internal IDs for three ownership buckets (`peter`, `maddie`, `joint`) and a distinct reporting scope (`combined`). Display names may change without changing IDs. Prefer `profileId` for ownership and `viewProfile` for the reporting selection; reserve existing budget `scope` for recurring versus monthly records.

1. **Account:** owns its balance and its default transaction attribution. Provider account identity remains item ID + account ID. Ownership is local metadata preserved across sync, reauthentication, and account updates.
2. **Investment:** default ownership follows its investment account. Individual holding overrides are supported because the user asked to tag investments; each position is owned exactly once. Brokerage cash follows the account. Preserve existing account-versus-holding valuation precedence so an account balance and its holdings are never both added. If mixed ownership cannot be reconciled against the provider's total, report the personal allocation as incomplete rather than fabricate it; Combined retains the known provider total once.
3. **Property/mortgage:** property has an owner bucket; mortgage defaults to its property bucket. Existing economic ownership percentages and liability shares still apply before household aggregation. These percentages are not a Peter/Maddie split. Validate linked liability accounts to prevent duplicate debt and inconsistent attribution; require an explicit resolution when ownership conflicts.
4. **Transaction:** effective profile = explicit local override or account profile. Include source/reason in detail UI. Import, sync, category changes, splits, undo, and reconciliation must preserve this metadata. New bank accounts/imports require an ownership choice or an explicit “Needs ownership review” state; do not silently assign new records based on who last opened the dashboard. An unresolved record appears once in Combined with a review notice and in neither personal view.
5. **Plans:** budget records, projected-income schedules, monthly overrides, outlooks, rollovers, goals, commitments, scenarios, allocation ledgers, and plan history have a profile dimension. Peter saves cannot overwrite Maddie's or Joint's records for the same month/category.
6. **Categories:** retain one shared system taxonomy for household comparison. Give custom categories a profile owner and stable ID; allow identical custom names in different profiles. Combined may group equivalent categories for display, but retains source IDs and contributor rows; never merge editable records by label. Adapt existing global uniqueness constraints and rename/archive semantics.
7. **Cross-profile relationships:** V1 goal funding, scenario-to-budget application, commitment matching, and ownership-dependent links must be compatible with their target profile. Block inconsistent new links with actionable guidance. Account moves preview affected goals, commitments, investments, and liability links and require reassignment or unlinking in the same transaction; no silent dangling relationships.
8. **Snapshots/audit:** store profile and attribution-policy metadata for new snapshots. Preserve old snapshots as legacy household/unknown-attribution evidence; do not invent personal snapshots. Separate authenticated actor from the financial owner of a record.

Combined reporting includes an explicit unresolved-ownership subtotal when applicable: Combined = Peter + Maddie + Joint + Needs review. With all records classified, the fourth subtotal is zero. Keep unknown values distinct from zero, disclose partial totals, and use the current posted/pending, refund, transfer, currency, and freshness rules consistently.

## Summary and charts

For the selected month, Combined Overview presents a comparison table with columns Peter, Maddie, Joint, and Household total. Rows: actual income, projected income, actual spending, planned spending, actual net cash flow, and planned surplus/shortfall. Show category plan versus actual with contributor drilldown. Personal views show the same metrics for their own records only. Net worth, account balances, property values, investments, and goals respect the selected view too.

Add two charts first:

- Monthly actual income and spending over the last 12 calendar months, with a household breakdown in Combined and a single profile in a personal view.
- Selected-month spending versus budget by category, with Peter/Maddie/Joint contributors available in Combined and an explicit distinction between observed spending and plans.

Charts must reconcile exactly to the corresponding table/query using the same selected dates and inclusion rules. Months without coverage are unknown/partial rather than invented zeroes. Use readable labels, accessible color contrast, keyboard-accessible details, and an equivalent table. Include loading, empty, missing-income-plan, stale-data, and partial-ownership states. Avoid pie charts comparing income with spending or graphing account balances as income.

Combined planning is a rollup, not an extra competing budget. Edits from Combined require an explicit Peter/Maddie/Joint target, shown in the editor and save confirmation. Editing a Joint plan changes the household total without changing either personal plan. Do not offer a writable aggregate total that distributes money automatically.
