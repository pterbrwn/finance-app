Method: dual-agent (A: ses_e4383adf6fferuI2THJ9zdVGrt · B: ses_e43838a7cffeQKBLKMQ2Z1voHw)

## Design specificity and overall impression

Ledger has a coherent warm off-white/deep-green visual system and concrete financial status language. It feels suited to private household finance. Its weakness is prioritization: populated household screens read like financial administration reports rather than quick daily guidance. The single biggest opportunity is making “What needs attention?” and “What remains in our plan?” obvious first.

## What works

- Three-step first-run guidance and clearly synthetic sample preview.
- Honest distinctions between missing, stale, projected and recorded financial data.
- Collapsed phone Transactions puts primary actions and real rows within reach; manual-entry fields are large and consistent.

## Priority issues

1. **P1 — Household Overview buries the daily answer.** `ui/browser.js`, overview()/householdComparison(). The populated household layout places comparisons and analysis before headline spending/remaining-plan information. Lead with monthly actuals, remaining category allowance and decisions needing attention; disclose profile/year comparisons below. Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`.
2. **P1 — Save recovery exposes implementation mechanics.** `ui/browser.js:60` refers to original revisions and save IDs. Explain uncertainty plainly: “We couldn’t confirm your budget saved.” Offer Check again / View saved budget, with technical detail separately. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
3. **P1 — Bills leads with setup instead of what is due.** `ui/browser.js:361–363` places creation before upcoming bills. Monthly frequency, first due date, due day and annual due month can coexist. Lead with upcoming bills, put creation behind Add bill, reveal frequency-specific inputs and preview the schedule. Suggested command: `$impeccable shape`.
4. **P2 — Budget and income figures need a clearer conceptual hierarchy.** Actual received income, planned take-home, remaining category allowance and planned surplus compete. Separate actual this month, plan allowance and planned month-end result. Simplify Household-versus-Joint entry points for shared-plan editing. These observations concern comprehension, not a demonstrated calculation error. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
5. **P2 — Rule editor misses established phone form styling.** `ui/browser.js:197`, `ui/styles.css:189–196`. Phone inputs measure 30px and selects 21px, compared with 44px manual-entry controls. Stack labels and full-width styled controls, explain signed amount filters, and require deliberate category selection rather than initially choosing Housing. Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt`.

## Personas and cognitive load

- First-time/nontechnical partner: Household versus Joint, multiple income meanings and technical recovery instructions increase hesitation.
- Distracted phone user: long reports and Bills setup delay the task; collapsed Transactions works well.
- Keyboard/low-vision user: visible focus and labeled controls help; small rule controls remain an ergonomic issue. No claim of a broken modal focus trap: background app focus escape was not demonstrated.

Cognitive load is high on populated Household Overview/Budgets: single focus, chunking, one decision at a time and progressive disclosure need work. Onboarding is welcoming; competing figures create uncertainty; technical recovery introduces anxiety.
